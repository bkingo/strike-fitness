# Crunch Fitness business and Lake Worth launch research

## 1. Research purpose and scope

Research date: October 9, 2026. This document examines the December 2024–January 2025 launch of Crunch Lake Worth as a reference for the fictional Strike Fitness portfolio project. It follows the scope and established decisions in `README.md`, `AGENTS.md`, and `docs/decisions.md`; it does not approve a Strike offer, positioning, or strategy.

**Evidence standard:** “Verified” means supported by the cited material, primarily contemporaneous company announcements. Announced features and scheduled events are not independent proof of delivery. Interpretations explain possible marketing effects; potential recommendations are options for later review. Current website information is explicitly separated from launch evidence. Source identifiers link directly to the URLs listed in section 10.

## 2. Crunch Fitness business overview

**Verified:** Crunch described itself in January 2025 as a high-value, low-price gym franchisor. Its brand combines exercise with entertainment, inclusive participation under its “No Judgments” philosophy, and distinctive group classes and programming. [S4]

The customer-facing model centers on recurring memberships. The current Lake Worth page illustrates tiered access through Base, Peak, and Peak Results, with personal training offered separately. This explains the general commercial structure, but does not establish the original launch tiers or their inclusions. [S3]

**Interpretation:** The proposition combines an accessible entry price with reasons to pay for a broader experience. Its appeal can span people who want independent workouts, social classes, or coaching rather than one narrowly defined training audience. The differentiator is the combination of variety, affordability, and a welcoming, entertaining brand experience. This is positioning analysis, not evidence about member demographics, retention, profitability, or the share of revenue from each service.

**Current fees and conditions:** [S3]

- Standard enrollment is $75 for each option. The live page data contains an automatically applied $1 enrollment promotion for Peak, Peak Results and Base's $15.99 monthly option; no expiry is supplied. Base's $9.99 commitment option retains $75 enrollment. These promotional amounts are not permanent fee policies.
- Standard and automatically promoted plans carry a $10 prorated annual fee at signup, a $59.99 recurring annual fee before tax ($63.89 in the tax-inclusive data), and a $2.25 recurring monthly processing fee before tax ($2.40 tax-inclusive). Advertised monthly dues exclude enrollment, processing, annual fees and applicable taxes. Do not treat the $10 signup charge as the full annual fee.
- For an October 9 signup, the data schedules the first recurring dues for November 9 and the annual fee for December 8, 2026. These are signup-date-specific examples. Automatic-promotion totals, including initial dues, $1 enrollment, $10 proration and tax, are $28.74 for Base monthly, $38.33 for Peak and $43.65 for Peak Results. Base with annual commitment shows $101.16 due initially without that promotion.
- Indexed Lake Worth cards label the monthly options month-to-month, with no commitment beginning **after an initial 90 days**; the live data also specifies a 90-day minimum. Base separately offers a 12-month commitment.

**Current linked agreement:** Its monthly clause specifies automatic monthly renewal and 30 days' written cancellation notice, delivered in person or by mail, with unpaid first-three-month dues owed for early cancellation. Another clause prohibits cancellation within the initial 90 days, subject to stated cancellation exceptions, and describes annual renewal after an initial term of at most 12 months. These provisions are not fully consistent; the completed plan-specific agreement is needed to resolve renewal and annual-commitment termination rules. Freezes expire after three months and do not suspend annual fees. Guest rules require registration/ID, accompaniment and a waiver; eligible guests receive Base access only, with one local guest per 24 hours and possible fees for out-of-town visitors. The advertised unlimited-guest wording does not establish unlimited simultaneous guests or specialty amenities. This is a current agreement template, not a recovered launch contract. [S8]

Treat these as FAQ-only claims requiring club confirmation; recreational pool access by tier is unspecified. Generic copy mentions steam rooms and classes for every membership, but the specific plan data does not establish steam-room availability or Base class access. Recovery session restrictions remain unknown. [S3]

## 6. Launch marketing and social media

**Marketing interpretations:** The campaign contains several distinct conversion ideas:

- The sale URL supplies a clear action destination. [S1]
- Time and quantity limits create urgency and scarcity. [S1]
- The later incentive encourages enrollment before opening. [S2]
- Non-member event access could reduce uncertainty before purchase. [S2]
- Local employment messaging extends the community benefit beyond members. [S2]

These mechanisms do not prove their effectiveness. The evidence does not establish conversion rates, membership totals, advertising spend, targeting, or which channel produced signups.

**Conversion evidence boundary:** Online membership purchase was explicitly encouraged, but the historical registration form and checkout were not recovered. No launch-specific evidence establishes a separate email waitlist, fitness-interest questionnaire, automated nurture sequence, remarketing audience, or segmentation program. Those would be possible future Strike choices, not reconstructed Crunch tactics.

## 7. Audience and value proposition

**Verified brand intent:** Crunch describes a community accommodating different people, goals, and exercise preferences. [S4] The Lake Worth operator emphasized inclusive participation, quality equipment, classes, personal training, and value. [S5]

**Audience interpretation:** Plausible benefit groups include price-sensitive exercisers, people seeking class variety, independent strength/cardio users, and people interested in coaching. These are analytical groupings, not verified campaign targeting or demographic findings. There is no basis here to choose a specific age, income band, gender, commute radius, or dominant customer segment for Strike.

The useful value-proposition lesson is that affordability can attract attention while concrete service choices explain why someone would use the club. A broad offering still requires clarity about what a particular membership buys.

## 8. Implications for Strike Fitness

**Potential recommendations for later review—not approved decisions:**

- Consider modeling the combination of accessible membership, multiple workout formats, coaching, and recovery. Select only facilities the fictional business will explicitly adopt, and keep membership inclusions clear.
- Use the documented launch incentives as references when evaluating an offer. Before adopting any mechanism, define the normal price, all charges, eligibility, expiry, and exactly what an early member receives.
- Consider whether a staged launch could serve the project: advance enrollment, a stated workout opening, and a community event can have distinct purposes. Fictional dates and event details still require direction.
- Learn from the relationship between a tangible facility promise, a clear action destination, and a credible reason to act promptly. Any capacity or deadline claim should have an explicit fictional business basis.

Before landing-page strategy, decisions remain needed on priority audience, selected facilities, tier inclusions, offer economics and conditions, launch timeline, and the primary conversion action. The established email and fitness-interest capture requirement comes from this project; this research does not demonstrate that Crunch used the same approach. Strike's identity, colors, voice, and conversion strategy remain open.

## 9. Research gaps and limitations

The strongest historical evidence is promotional material issued before opening. It establishes what was advertised, not independent confirmation of delivery or customer response. The supplied screenshots add campaign context, but the rate-reservation page behavior, complete offer terms, tax treatment of the $1.07 amount, and actual conversion results remain unverified.

The December 12 PR Newswire URL repeatedly failed direct retrieval, but its indexed article text was available through web search. The SGB page returned a direct-access 403; its indexed text was accessible. The December 27 release and current Crunch page were readable. No current promotion has been used as a launch offer.

Most useful remaining evidence would be the original membership agreement/checkout, dated local campaign assets, and a post-opening report. Those could resolve fee obligations, feature access, creative execution, and actual opening activity. Until then, do not assume a lowest-price membership included every amenity, that all promised perks were delivered, or that any particular marketing tactic succeeded.
