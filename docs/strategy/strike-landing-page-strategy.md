# Strike Fitness Landing-Page Strategy

**Status:** Strategic recommendation  
**Company:** Strike Fitness, a fictional independent fitness company  
**Document date:** October 2026  
**Primary purpose:** Pre-launch and early-stage lead generation  
**Strategic source of truth:** `docs/strategy/strike-business-brand-brief.md`

## Strategic Context and Boundaries

This landing page is the first public conversion layer for Strike Fitness. It should translate the business strategy into a focused value exchange: a relevant local prospect gives Strike permission to communicate in return for useful launch information and access to appropriate pre-launch opportunities.

The page must reflect Strike's actual stage. Strike has no operating location, final site, confirmed opening date, final pricing, finished facility, or proven member outcomes. The page may explain the proposed concept and invite interest, but it must not present planning assumptions as established facts.

This document defines page purpose, content structure, form design, data strategy, segmentation, lifecycle implications, trust requirements, and measurement. It does not provide final landing-page copy, visual design, application code, email copy, or an ESP recommendation.

## 1. Landing Page Objective

The landing page should build a permission-based list of relevant prospects while helping Strike learn which parts of its proposed fitness experience create demand.

Its job is to:

1. Make the Strike concept understandable quickly.
2. Establish a credible point of difference from access-only gyms and rigid boutique studios.
3. Give visitors a concrete reason to receive communications now, before final membership details are available.
4. Capture the minimum information needed to identify, contact, and initially personalize a lead.
5. Preserve source, consent, and signup context for future lifecycle marketing and funnel analysis.
6. Set accurate expectations about Strike's pre-launch status.

The page should optimize for **qualified, permissioned interest**, not the lowest possible cost per email address. A smaller list of local prospects who understand the concept is more useful than a large list generated through vague giveaways or misleading discounts.

## 2. Target Visitor

### Primary visitor

The primary visitor is a routine-seeking adult who wants to train two to four times per week but struggles with inconsistency, uncertainty, or impersonal fitness experiences. This person may have used gyms, apps, home workouts, or classes before. The central problem is not lack of awareness that exercise matters; it is creating a realistic and repeatable training rhythm.

Typical needs include:

- More direction than an access-only gym provides.
- More flexibility than a single-format studio provides.
- A clear way to begin or resume without pressure.
- Confidence that equipment, coaching, schedule, and space will fit real life.
- Straightforward information about price, policies, and availability once those details are confirmed.

### Important secondary visitors

- Self-directed strength and functional-training prospects who care about equipment quality and usable space.
- Former boutique members who want coaching and group energy with greater flexibility.
- Beginners and returning exercisers who want a low-anxiety starting point.
- Active adults rebuilding strength, mobility, or stamina within appropriate general-fitness scope.

The initial page should not attempt to create separate demographic experiences for each audience. It should use one coherent promise and let fitness-interest selection provide the first layer of relevance.

### Geographic qualification

Strike's eventual business depends on a local trade area, but no site or city is confirmed. The page should therefore avoid implying a location that has not been selected. Once a site is established, local relevance should become explicit in the page context and acquisition targeting.

ZIP code should not be added to the initial form before there is a defined trade area and a clear decision that depends on it. Until then, campaign targeting, referral context, and later engagement can provide directional evidence without asking every visitor for more data.

## 3. Primary Conversion

The primary conversion is:

> A visitor submits their first name, email address, and one primary fitness interest to receive relevant Strike Fitness launch updates and opportunities.

The value exchange may include, as those items become real:

- Meaningful progress and opening updates.
- Notice when tours, previews, pop-up sessions, or memberships become available.
- Information relevant to the visitor's stated fitness interest.
- Invitations to provide input on appropriate pre-launch decisions.

The page should not promise all of these benefits on every submission unless Strike is prepared to deliver them. The form context should identify the actual communication expectation at launch.

Success is not merely a browser-side form submission. A successful primary conversion requires:

- Valid contact data.
- Clear permission and disclosure.
- A lead record successfully created or updated in the system of record.
- Source, signup timestamp, and consent context preserved.
- A confirmation state that accurately tells the visitor what happens next.

## 4. Secondary Conversion

A separate business conversion is not necessary for the first version. Competing calls to purchase, book, apply, or follow social accounts would dilute the lead-generation objective when those paths are not yet mature.

The appropriate secondary action is an on-page **learn-more micro-conversion** for visitors who are interested but not ready to submit. It can move the visitor to details about:

- How Strike differs from a conventional gym or boutique studio.
- The proposed training experience.
- What subscribers can expect.
- How Strike will handle pricing, capacity, and sales behavior.

This action should keep visitors on the page and return them naturally to the same lead form. It should not be styled with the same prominence as the primary CTA.

Once real events, tours, or previews exist, registration can become a contextual secondary conversion. Those offers should not be fabricated merely to give the page another CTA.

## 5. Core Value Proposition

The landing page should express the strategic proposition:

**Strike Fitness is being built to make consistency easier by combining the freedom of a gym with clear, flexible guidance.**

Supporting value themes:

- **Clarity:** A member should understand how to begin and what to do next.
- **Flexibility:** Independent training, guided sessions, and optional coaching can fit different needs and changing schedules.
- **Usability:** The proposed club prioritizes practical training space, focused programming, and capacity awareness over a long amenity list.
- **Respect:** Communications, sales, pricing, and support should be candid and low pressure.
- **Local relevance:** Strike is intended to become a useful neighborhood fitness business, not a generic campaign with a local label.

The proposition must be framed as an intended experience until operational details are confirmed. The page should not imply that Strike has already delivered these outcomes.

## 6. Messaging Hierarchy

The page should communicate in this order:

1. **Visitor problem:** Access alone often does not create a consistent routine, while rigid or expensive formats can be difficult to sustain.
2. **Strike's answer:** A neighborhood fitness concept combining open-gym freedom, clear training paths, and flexible levels of support.
3. **Practical benefit:** Visitors can find a realistic way to train, know their next step, and adjust support as needs change.
4. **Who it is for:** Adults seeking consistency, strength, conditioning, mobility, coaching, or a sensible way to restart.
5. **Why engage now:** Receive relevant updates and first notice of real launch, preview, and membership opportunities.
6. **Credibility and stage:** Strike is new and in development; confirmed details will be shared as they become real.
7. **Trust:** No invented outcomes, false scarcity, vague teaser pricing, or high-pressure promise.
8. **Conversion:** Submit minimal information and choose the fitness interest most relevant to future communications.

This hierarchy should avoid leading with a generic newsletter invitation. The visitor must first understand the problem Strike intends to solve and why future communication will be worth receiving.

## 7. Recommended Page Sections

### 1. Hero and primary value exchange

Purpose:

- Establish the problem and differentiated concept.
- State that Strike is pre-launch or in development.
- Present the primary CTA.
- Set a clear expectation for what signup provides.

The hero should not use an unverified location, opening date, price, facility rendering, or membership offer.

### 2. The gap Strike is designed to fill

Explain the middle ground between:

- Low-cost or access-only gyms that offer freedom but little direction.
- Boutique studios that offer coaching but may be expensive, narrow, or schedule-dependent.

This section should clarify category position without attacking competitors or claiming superiority that has not been proven.

### 3. How the proposed experience works

Describe the connected model at a strategic level:

- Open gym for independent training.
- Simple training paths for direction.
- Focused coached sessions.
- Optional higher-touch support.

This should explain the system, not publish final memberships or programming that remain under validation.

### 4. Experience principles

Use concise proof-oriented themes based on the brand promise:

- A clear next step.
- Space designed for useful training.
- Support without pressure.
- Transparent information as details are confirmed.

These principles are more defensible than an exhaustive amenity list.

### 5. Interest and fit

Help visitors recognize themselves through needs rather than demographics. The section can frame relevant paths such as building strength, using group guidance, improving conditioning, moving better, or getting back into a routine.

This section should prepare visitors for the form's interest selection and make the choices easier to understand.

### 6. What subscribers can expect

State the actual communication categories and expected benefit of joining the list. This is essential to permission quality. Potential categories include progress updates, opportunities to preview the concept, and information related to the selected interest.

Do not promise a fixed cadence, discount, event, or priority status unless it can be honored.

### 7. Trust and frequently asked questions

Address the most likely uncertainties:

- Is Strike open yet?
- Where will it be?
- When will it open?
- What will membership cost?
- Is the list a membership commitment?
- What happens after signup?
- How will personal information be used?

Unknowns should be answered as unknowns, paired with how and when Strike intends to communicate confirmed information.

### 8. Lead form and final CTA

Repeat the core value exchange near the end of the page. On shorter mobile experiences, an earlier form may be appropriate, but the final CTA should remain available after objections have been addressed.

### 9. Minimal footer

Include contact or company identification as appropriate, privacy information, communication expectations, and unsubscribe reassurance. Avoid navigation that creates unnecessary exits unless legally or operationally required.

## 8. Recommended CTA Strategy

### Primary CTA

Use one consistent action concept throughout the page: join the permission-based Strike interest list for relevant updates and opportunities.

CTA principles:

- Describe the benefit or next step, not merely “Submit.”
- Keep the commitment level accurate; joining an interest list is not joining the gym.
- Use the same primary action in the hero, after key explanatory content, and near the final form.
- On long mobile pages, a restrained sticky CTA may link to the form rather than opening an unexpected flow.
- Preserve the visitor's progress if repeated CTAs scroll or open the form.

### Secondary CTA

Use a visually subordinate link to learn how Strike is intended to work or to review common questions. The destination should remain on-page.

### CTA rules

- Do not use fake countdowns, repeated “last chance” language, or quantity scarcity without a real operational constraint.
- Do not advertise a founder rate, discount, price, or opening date before it is validated.
- Do not describe list subscribers as members.
- Do not make a social follow, outbound navigation, or content download visually compete with the form.
- When a genuine preview, event, or enrollment offer exists, give it a distinct CTA tied to a specific lifecycle stage.

## 9. Lead Form Strategy

### Recommended first-touch form

| Field | Recommendation | Strategic reason |
|---|---|---|
| First name | **Required** | Enables a more human confirmation and lifecycle experience with low sensitivity and relatively low effort. |
| Last name | **Removed** | Adds friction and identification data without changing initial messaging, routing, or offer eligibility. |
| Email address | **Required** | Necessary to deliver the promised communications and serve as the initial profile identifier. |
| Primary fitness interest | **Required** | Creates immediate relevance, supports segmentation, and gives Strike structured demand-learning data. |
| Secondary fitness interest | **Optional** | Captures a meaningful second motivation without forcing visitors to over-specify; the interface must enforce a maximum of two total interests. |

Final visible fields: **first name, email address, primary fitness interest, and optional secondary fitness interest**.

No additional visible field is recommended for the initial form.

### Field-by-field evaluation

#### First name — required

**Conversion perspective:**  
First name creates a small amount of friction, but it is familiar, easy to answer, and materially less burdensome than full name. The form remains short.

**Marketing/CRM perspective:**  
It supports respectful personalization in confirmations and lifecycle messages and gives staff a usable conversational identifier if a lead later requests human contact.

**Segmentation/personalization perspective:**  
It is useful for message personalization but should not be treated as a segmentation variable.

**Data-minimization perspective:**  
The use is immediate and clear. Strike should collect only the preferred first name supplied and should not infer legal identity.

**Recommendation:** Required. If testing later shows a material completion penalty, email-only capture can be tested, but the strategic default is to retain first name.

#### Last name — removed

**Conversion perspective:**  
It adds another input and can make a low-commitment interest form feel more like an enrollment or sales-lead form.

**Marketing/CRM perspective:**  
It may help with deduplication or staff workflows later, but email can serve as the initial identifier. Full name becomes more relevant at event registration, tour booking, enrollment, or account creation.

**Segmentation/personalization perspective:**  
It provides no useful initial segment and no meaningful content relevance.

**Data-minimization perspective:**  
There is no immediate need strong enough to justify collecting it “just in case.”

**Recommendation:** Remove from the first-touch form. Collect later only when a specific operational process requires it.

#### Email address — required

**Conversion perspective:**  
Email is the central ask and expected cost of the value exchange. The page must make the benefit and communication expectation clear enough to justify it.

**Marketing/CRM perspective:**  
It is the primary communication channel and initial unique profile key. Validation should catch obvious format errors without creating aggressive correction loops.

**Segmentation/personalization perspective:**  
Email itself is not a segment. Delivery, engagement, consent, and lifecycle behavior associated with the profile can later support responsible messaging decisions.

**Data-minimization perspective:**  
It is necessary for the stated purpose. It should not be exposed in analytics payloads or used for unrelated communications.

**Recommendation:** Required.

#### Primary fitness interest — required

**Conversion perspective:**  
A single-choice question adds friction relative to name and email alone, but a short, understandable taxonomy can feel useful because it signals that future communication will be relevant. The form should make selection quick on mobile.

**Marketing/CRM perspective:**  
It creates an actionable profile property, helps prioritize future content and event concepts, and provides structured demand evidence.

**Segmentation/personalization perspective:**  
This is the highest-value initial segmentation field. It can influence educational emphasis, preview invitations, and objection handling.

**Data-minimization perspective:**  
The field is justified only because each option maps to a planned communication or learning use. It must stay broad and should not request health conditions, body measurements, diagnoses, or sensitive goals.

**Recommendation:** Required.

#### Secondary fitness interest — optional

**Conversion perspective:**  
Requiring two choices would force false precision, increase decision effort, and disadvantage visitors with one clear priority. An optional second selection accommodates mixed motivations without blocking completion.

**Marketing/CRM perspective:**  
It can enrich content selection and reveal useful combinations, such as strength training plus personal guidance or cardio workouts plus group classes.

**Segmentation/personalization perspective:**  
It should refine—not override—the primary interest. A second interest can support secondary content modules, event recommendations, and cross-interest analysis.

**Data-minimization perspective:**  
The information has plausible near-term use, but it is not essential. Leaving it optional respects uncertainty and keeps the required data set small.

**Recommendation:** Optional. Reveal or enable it only after the primary choice, make “add a second interest” clearly optional, and enforce a maximum of two total selections.

### Form interaction recommendations

- Present interests as large single-select controls for the primary choice.
- Give each consumer-facing label a short helper line with familiar examples. The examples should clarify the category without implying that an unconfirmed program or amenity will be offered.
- After selection, offer an optional second choice using the same taxonomy, excluding the primary selection.
- State “Choose one, or up to two” near the interest control.
- If a two-selection multi-select interface is used instead, require the visitor to designate which interest is primary. Do not infer primacy from visual order unless that behavior is explicit.
- Do not preselect an interest.
- Do not require a visitor to choose “Other” and then type a free-text answer. Use behavior and later research to determine whether the taxonomy needs revision.
- Use inline validation and retain entered values after an error.
- Include concise permission and privacy context adjacent to the submission control.
- Do not add a marketing-consent checkbox solely by habit. The submission experience should clearly state what the person is signing up for. If a checkbox or jurisdiction-specific consent treatment is legally required, it should be explicit, unbundled, and recorded.
- Confirm successful completion only after the lead has been accepted by the lead system, or clearly handle delayed processing without falsely claiming success.

## 10. Fitness Interest Taxonomy

### Recommended consumer-facing taxonomy

1. **Strength training**  
   Free weights, weight machines, and functional strength workouts
2. **Cardio workouts**  
   Treadmills, stair climbers, bikes, and other cardio training
3. **Group fitness classes**  
   Instructor-led strength, cardio, and movement classes
4. **Yoga & Pilates**  
   Yoga, Pilates, stretching, and mobility-focused sessions
5. **Personal training & guidance**  
   Individual or small-group help with getting started, making progress, or staying consistent

Visitors may choose one primary interest and, optionally, one secondary interest.

### Why these options exist

#### Strength training

“Strength training” is a familiar umbrella term. The helper text makes weight-based methods explicit without making the label redundantly say both “strength” and “weight” training. It represents independent or coached strength development, including foundational strength, free weights, machines, and functional training.

Potential future relevance:

- Equipment and training-space information.
- Strength-path education.
- Technique clinics or strength-focused previews.
- Differentiation from a class-only studio.

#### Cardio workouts

“Cardio workouts” is more recognizable to everyday visitors than “conditioning.” It includes prospects primarily interested in cardiovascular equipment as well as broader stamina-oriented training without requiring them to understand sports-performance terminology.

Potential future relevance:

- Cardio-equipment and space information when confirmed.
- Stamina and cardiovascular-fitness education within appropriate fitness scope.
- Cardio-oriented previews, workshops, or programming when available.
- Demand evidence for equipment mix and programming decisions.

#### Group fitness classes

“Group fitness classes” is a familiar consumer category for people seeking instructor guidance, scheduled accountability, and shared energy. The helper text keeps the category broad without suggesting that every common class format will be available.

Potential future relevance:

- Coached-session explanations.
- Sample schedules and preview opportunities when confirmed.
- Group accountability and class-experience information.
- Signals related to likely interest in a Guided membership concept.

#### Yoga & Pilates

“Yoga & Pilates” uses formats consumers are more likely to recognize than the standalone terms “mobility” or “movement.” The helper text connects those familiar formats to stretching and mobility while avoiding medical or rehabilitative claims.

Potential future relevance:

- Yoga-, Pilates-, stretching-, or mobility-related demand validation.
- Information about the proposed Restore programming pillar.
- Relevant previews or workshops when confirmed.
- Content for prospects balancing other training with flexibility and movement quality.

#### Personal training & guidance

“Personal training & guidance” describes a recognizable type of support rather than asking visitors to classify themselves as beginners or routine rebuilders. It includes individual or small-group help for people who want technique instruction, accountability, adaptation, or a clear starting point.

Potential future relevance:

- Explanations of optional one-to-one or semi-private coaching.
- Technique, accountability, and onboarding information.
- Appropriate consultations or starter experiences when available.
- Signals related to interest in a higher-support membership or service.

### Taxonomy rationale

Five options are enough to distinguish important preferences without turning the form into a program catalog. The taxonomy:

- Uses familiar consumer language instead of internal fitness terminology such as “conditioning” or an abstract phrase such as “mobility and movement.”
- Distinguishes independent workout interests, group-class interest, and higher-touch guidance.
- Lets examples do the explanatory work instead of making every label long or technically exhaustive.
- Keeps routine-building central to Strike's overall positioning without incorrectly presenting “rebuild a routine” as a fitness activity.
- Avoids sensitive or appearance-led choices such as weight loss, injury recovery, medical improvement, or body transformation.
- Can map to future content and offers rather than existing only for reporting.

Some natural overlap remains. A visitor may be interested in both cardio workouts and group fitness classes, or both Yoga & Pilates and group fitness classes. The optional second selection is designed to preserve that nuance rather than force every person into one mutually exclusive category.

The labels and examples describe areas of interest, not a confirmed Strike class or amenity catalog. Before publication, Strike must verify that every named example is genuinely under consideration and appropriate to the concept. In particular, Yoga, Pilates, treadmills, stair climbers, and cycling should not be presented in a way that promises availability before facility and programming decisions are confirmed. If Strike decides not to pursue a named format, the example or category should be revised before launch.

Strike should monitor selection behavior and downstream engagement. It should revise the taxonomy when evidence shows confusion, excessive concentration, or a repeated need that the choices do not represent.

## 11. Lead/Profile Data Model

### Information collected directly from the visitor

| Property | Collection | Profile treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `first_name` | Required visible field | Store as a profile property for respectful personalization. |
| `email` | Required visible field | Store as the primary contact identifier and communication address. |
| `primary_interest` | Required visible field | Store as a controlled profile property using stable internal values. |
| `secondary_interest` | Optional visible field | Store as a nullable controlled profile property. |
| `email_marketing_consent` | Captured through the explicit signup action and applicable consent control | Store as a permission status, not merely a campaign tag. |

The interface labels can evolve without changing the underlying controlled values. For example, a visitor-facing label may be refined while an internal value such as `strength` remains stable.

### Information captured automatically

| Property | Capture method | Profile treatment |
|---|---|---|
| `lead_source` | Page and acquisition context | Store as first-touch source using a defined source taxonomy. |
| `signup_date` | Server-side timestamp at successful creation | Store as an immutable or auditable profile property. |
| `signup_page` | Current page identifier or URL path | Store as acquisition context. |
| `campaign_source`, `campaign_medium`, `campaign_name` | Valid campaign parameters when present | Store as attribution properties; preserve raw values where appropriate. |
| `referrer_category` | Derived from available referral context | Store a normalized category where useful; avoid unnecessary full-URL retention. |
| `consent_timestamp` | Server-side timestamp | Store in the consent record. |
| `consent_language_version` | Version identifier for the disclosure shown | Store in the consent record. |
| `profile_created_at` / `profile_updated_at` | System timestamps | Maintain for data governance and lifecycle logic. |

Automatic fields should not be exposed as editable visitor fields. `lead_source` and `signup_date`, in particular, should never be requested from the visitor.

### Information derived later

Derived properties should be based on observed behavior or later voluntary interactions and should remain distinct from stated preferences.

Potential derived properties:

- `lifecycle_stage`: new lead, engaged lead, event registrant, attendee, tour lead, enrollment lead, member, or inactive.
- `engagement_level`: based on defined and time-bounded communication or site behaviors.
- `interest_combination`: a reporting or decision value derived from primary and secondary interests.
- `content_affinity`: based on repeated engagement with a topic, not one click.
- `acquisition_channel_group`: normalized from source and campaign data.
- `lead_readiness`: inferred cautiously from high-intent actions such as event registration, tour requests, or enrollment activity.
- `trade_area_status`: only after a site exists and sufficient location information is voluntarily obtained through an appropriate later interaction.

Derived data must not silently replace explicit data. For example, reading strength content should not overwrite a visitor's stated primary interest.

### Information that should not be collected on the initial form

- Last name.
- Phone number or SMS consent.
- Full address, ZIP code, or neighborhood before a location-dependent use exists.
- Date of birth, age, gender, income, occupation, household composition, or employer.
- Weight, body measurements, body-composition goals, photographs, or transformation history.
- Diagnoses, medications, injuries, disabilities, medical history, or rehabilitation status.
- Preferred workout time, experience level, current gym, budget, membership tier, or purchase readiness.
- Open-ended “tell us about your goals” text.
- Password or account credentials.

Some non-sensitive items may become appropriate in a later event, research, tour, or enrollment context. They do not belong in the first-touch lead form because they do not improve the immediate value exchange enough to justify added friction.

### Storage principles

- Store first name, email, interests, acquisition context, lifecycle stage, and consent state as marketing profile properties or associated auditable records.
- Use controlled enumerations for interests, lifecycle stages, and normalized sources.
- Keep stated attributes distinct from inferred attributes.
- Treat blank secondary interest as a valid state, not missing required data.
- Define how profile updates, duplicate emails, unsubscribes, deletion requests, and suppression are handled before activation.
- Do not send raw email addresses, names, or sensitive values to general analytics tools.
- Establish retention and access rules before collecting data at scale.

## 12. Segmentation Strategy

Initial segmentation should remain simple and action-oriented.

### Primary segmentation dimensions

#### Stated primary interest

This is the main content-relevance segment:

- Strength training.
- Cardio workouts.
- Group fitness classes.
- Yoga & Pilates.
- Personal training & guidance.

#### Secondary interest

Use as a refinement rather than a separate competing identity. It can influence secondary content, invitation prioritization, or analysis of common combinations.

#### Lifecycle stage

At minimum:

- New lead.
- Engaged lead.
- Event or preview registrant.
- Event or preview attendee.
- High-intent lead, based on a defined action.
- Enrollment started.
- Member.
- Unsubscribed or suppressed.

Lifecycle stage should be behavior-based and system-managed, not asked on the first form.

#### Acquisition source

Normalize useful source groups such as:

- Organic search.
- Paid search.
- Paid social.
- Organic social.
- Partner or community referral.
- Event or pop-up.
- On-site or offline QR.
- Direct or unknown.

Do not let campaign naming create an unmanageable number of permanent profile segments.

### Segmentation rules

- A segment must change content, timing, offer, or analysis; otherwise it should not exist.
- Primary interest can change the emphasis of a message, but essential launch facts should remain consistent for everyone.
- Do not infer sensitive characteristics from interest selection.
- Do not treat “get started” as lower value or use it for more aggressive selling.
- Avoid tiny cross-segments until list size and use cases justify them.
- Keep operational messages, consent messages, and critical launch changes independent from marketing-interest suppression where legally appropriate.
- Allow stated preferences to be updated later.

## 13. Initial Lifecycle Marketing Implications

The form design creates a foundation for relevance without requiring a complex automation program.

### Immediate confirmation

The confirmation experience should:

- Confirm that the signup succeeded.
- Restate what communications the visitor can expect.
- Reflect the selected primary interest without overpersonalizing.
- Clarify that signup is not a membership commitment.
- Set expectations honestly if location, pricing, or timing is not yet confirmed.

### New-lead introduction

The first lifecycle stage should explain:

- The problem Strike is designed to solve.
- The intended balance of freedom and guidance.
- What is known, what remains in development, and why the visitor's interest matters.
- How to unsubscribe or update preferences.

### Interest-based education

Primary interest can determine which aspect of the concept receives emphasis:

- Strength training: equipment, space, technique, and progression principles once confirmed.
- Cardio workouts: cardio options and stamina-oriented programming once confirmed.
- Group fitness classes: coaching approach, class structure, and schedule logic.
- Yoga & Pilates: relevant programming, stretching, and mobility information without medical claims.
- Personal training & guidance: optional coaching, onboarding, technique, and accountability support.

This should be modular personalization, not five entirely separate brand narratives.

### Pre-launch progress

All leads may receive genuinely meaningful milestones such as:

- Site or opening-window confirmation.
- Facility and equipment decisions.
- Introduction of qualified staff.
- Preview, pop-up, or listening opportunities.
- Confirmed membership and policy information.

Updates should be sent because the information is useful, not merely to maintain a marketing cadence.

### Higher-intent transitions

Future actions such as registering for an event, requesting a tour, attending a preview, or starting enrollment should move the person into a more relevant lifecycle path. Strike should collect additional data only at the moment it supports that action.

### Preference evolution

The initial interest is a starting signal, not a permanent identity. Later preference centers, event choices, and meaningful behavior may refine the profile. Explicit changes should take precedence over old assumptions.

## 14. Trust and Credibility Strategy

Because Strike is new and has no operating history, credibility must come from specificity, transparency, and visible progress rather than borrowed scale or invented social proof.

### Stage transparency

- State clearly that Strike is in development and not yet open.
- Separate confirmed facts from proposed features.
- Use realistic timing language and update it promptly.
- Avoid displaying a location, price, schedule, rendering, or opening date as final until it is final.

### Concrete proof as it becomes available

Add evidence progressively:

- Founder rationale and relevant experience.
- Confirmed site and local context.
- Real build progress.
- Confirmed equipment and space decisions.
- Coach credentials and roles.
- Actual pop-up or community activity.
- Clear membership terms and policies.

Do not use stock imagery in a way that implies it depicts a completed Strike facility or actual members.

### Operational trust

Make Strike's principles legible:

- Clear total pricing when pricing is available.
- Straightforward terms and cancellation.
- Capacity-conscious planning.
- Respectful sales behavior.
- Accurate schedules and status updates.
- Clear handling of delays.

These should be framed as standards Strike intends to implement and later substantiated through actual policies.

### Data trust

- Explain what signup provides.
- Link to an understandable privacy notice.
- Record consent context.
- Avoid unrelated follow-up.
- Make unsubscribe and preference changes simple.
- Do not sell or broadly repurpose lead data.

### Social proof

Do not invent testimonials, membership counts, partner logos, reviews, or outcome claims. When genuine proof exists, use it with permission and enough context to avoid overstating what it demonstrates.

## 15. Objection Handling

### “I have joined gyms before and stopped going.”

Strategic response: Explain that Strike is being designed around clear starting paths, flexible support, and building a repeatable rhythm—not access alone or guilt-based motivation.

### “I do not know what to do in a gym.”

Strategic response: Describe guidance by default, approachable onboarding, and a clear next step without implying that personal training is mandatory.

### “I already know how to train.”

Strategic response: Show that independent training is a real part of the model and that guidance is available rather than forced. Future proof should include credible equipment and capacity information.

### “I cannot commit to one class schedule.”

Strategic response: Emphasize the planned combination of open-gym flexibility and focused coached options. Do not imply a breadth of schedule that has not been confirmed.

### “This sounds more expensive than a chain gym.”

Strategic response: Explain the intended added value—guidance, usability, and support—without publishing speculative pricing or claiming savings. Once pricing is final, show total cost and inclusions clearly.

### “Is this just another intense fitness concept?”

Strategic response: Use grounded visuals and language, explain modification and different entry points, and avoid combat, punishment, body-shame, or all-or-nothing cues.

### “Where and when will it open?”

Strategic response: State the current status honestly. Invite updates because confirmed location and timing are part of what subscribers will receive. Do not use ambiguity to imply that an opening is closer than it is.

### “Will signing up lead to sales pressure?”

Strategic response: Clarify the communication purpose, low-commitment nature of the list, and unsubscribe control. Follow-through must match the promise.

### “What will you do with my information?”

Strategic response: Explain that contact information is used to deliver requested updates and that selected interests help make those updates more relevant. Provide accessible privacy information.

## 16. Conversion Risks

### Weak value exchange

“Join our newsletter” is not sufficient. Visitors need to know what useful access or information they will receive.

**Mitigation:** Tie signup to concrete categories of future value without promising unconfirmed offers.

### Concept ambiguity

The hybrid of open gym, structured paths, and coaching may sound like an ordinary gym with higher prices.

**Mitigation:** Explain how the parts connect to solve inconsistency and uncertainty.

### Too much pre-launch uncertainty

Without a site, date, price, or finished product, visitors may defer action.

**Mitigation:** Be candid about the stage and provide a credible reason to follow progress. As proof becomes available, update the page.

### Premature specificity

Publishing speculative amenities, schedules, prices, or timelines can increase short-term conversion but create future trust failure.

**Mitigation:** Label proposals accurately and convert them to confirmed facts only when operationally supported.

### Form friction

Too many identity, qualification, or research questions can make a first-touch form feel intrusive.

**Mitigation:** Limit required fields to first name, email, and one interest. Keep the second interest optional and remove last name.

### Interest-choice confusion

Visitors may see overlap between workout types and support formats, such as cardio workouts and group fitness classes.

**Mitigation:** Use short helper descriptions in the interface, allow one optional second interest, test comprehension, and preserve a maximum of five choices.

### Generic fitness language

Category-standard claims can make Strike indistinguishable from chains and studios.

**Mitigation:** Center the specific problem of consistency and connect claims to proposed operating choices.

### Brand-name intimidation

“Strike” can imply aggression, combat, or intensity.

**Mitigation:** Pair the name with grounded language and inclusive visual direction; avoid violent wordplay and punishment themes.

### Unsubstantiated trust signals

Stock imagery, fake scarcity, invented testimonials, or implied facility proof may raise conversion temporarily while undermining credibility.

**Mitigation:** Use real proof only and make development status visible.

### Low-quality lead acquisition

Broad giveaways or discount-led ads may produce inexpensive but poorly matched leads.

**Mitigation:** Align acquisition messaging with the same routine-and-guidance proposition used on the page and evaluate downstream engagement, not form completion alone.

### Overpersonalization from limited data

One selected interest does not reveal a complete goal, identity, ability, or purchase intent.

**Mitigation:** Use interest to adjust emphasis, not to make unsupported assumptions.

### Measurement failure

Client-side form completion can be mistaken for a successfully created lead, or duplicate events can inflate performance.

**Mitigation:** Distinguish submission from backend creation, use stable event definitions, and reconcile analytics with the lead system.

## 17. Analytics and Events We Should Eventually Track

### Event naming principles

- Use lower-case `snake_case`.
- Name events for completed user or system actions, not interface labels.
- Keep event names stable; put page, placement, interest, and source details in properties.
- Separate browser behavior from backend lead outcomes.
- Do not include name, email address, or other direct identifiers in general analytics event properties.
- Define one canonical firing rule, owner, and deduplication method for every event.

### Recommended event taxonomy

#### `landing_page_viewed`

Fires when the page becomes meaningfully viewable, using a documented rule rather than every route initialization.

What it should tell us:

- Eligible page traffic.
- Channel and campaign mix.
- Device and page-variant context.
- The denominator for page conversion.

Useful non-PII properties:

- `page_variant`
- `device_category`
- `source_group`
- `campaign_name`
- `referrer_category`

#### `primary_cta_clicked`

Fires when a visitor uses a primary CTA to reach or open the form.

What it should tell us:

- Whether the value proposition creates intent before form interaction.
- Which CTA placement contributes to engagement.
- Whether visitors need more page content before acting.

Useful properties:

- `cta_placement`
- `page_variant`

#### `lead_form_started`

Fires once per page session when the visitor first meaningfully interacts with the form, not merely when the form enters the viewport.

What it should tell us:

- The gap between CTA or page interest and actual form engagement.
- Form-start rate by page variant and source.

Useful properties:

- `form_id`
- `start_field`

#### `fitness_interest_selected`

Fires when an interest is selected or changed.

What it should tell us:

- Distribution of stated demand.
- Whether visitors use the optional second selection.
- Whether repeated changes suggest taxonomy confusion.

Useful properties:

- `interest_value`
- `interest_priority` (`primary` or `secondary`)
- `selection_action` (`selected`, `changed`, or `removed`)

Interest values are non-sensitive controlled categories. They should still be used only for the documented purpose.

#### `lead_form_validation_failed`

Fires when submission is blocked by validation. Avoid firing on every keystroke.

What it should tell us:

- Which requirements cause preventable friction.
- Whether email formatting, missing interest, or technical behavior is reducing completion.

Useful properties:

- `error_type`
- `field_name`
- `error_count`

Do not capture the invalid field value.

#### `lead_form_submitted`

Fires when the browser sends a valid form for processing.

What it should tell us:

- Visitor completion intent.
- The size of the gap between valid submissions and successful lead creation.

Useful properties:

- `form_id`
- `has_secondary_interest`
- `page_variant`

This event is not the primary conversion metric because backend processing can still fail.

#### `lead_created`

Fires from the server or integration layer when a new profile is successfully created and consent/source context is stored.

What it should tell us:

- True lead acquisition volume.
- Successful landing-page conversion.
- New-profile creation by source and page variant.

Useful properties:

- `anonymous_conversion_id`
- `source_group`
- `page_variant`
- `primary_interest`
- `has_secondary_interest`
- `record_outcome` (`created`)

Use a non-PII conversion identifier for reconciliation.

#### `lead_updated`

Fires when an existing profile submits again and is successfully updated rather than duplicated.

What it should tell us:

- Repeat signup behavior.
- Preference updates.
- The difference between gross form conversions and net-new leads.

Useful properties:

- `anonymous_conversion_id`
- `update_type`
- `record_outcome` (`updated`)

#### `lead_creation_failed`

Fires when a valid submission cannot be written after appropriate processing.

What it should tell us:

- Integration reliability.
- Potential lead loss.
- Whether form experience and backend success diverge.

Useful properties:

- `failure_category`
- `integration_step`
- `retryable`

Do not expose internal error detail or personal data in client analytics.

#### `signup_confirmed`

Fires when the visitor is shown a genuine successful confirmation state after lead creation or update.

What it should tell us:

- Whether the complete user experience finished.
- Whether technical success was communicated successfully.

### Recommended funnel

Evaluate:

1. `landing_page_viewed`
2. `primary_cta_clicked` or direct form engagement
3. `lead_form_started`
4. `lead_form_submitted`
5. `lead_created` or `lead_updated`
6. `signup_confirmed`

Primary conversion rate should use successful `lead_created` plus valid `lead_updated` outcomes according to the reporting question, not raw button clicks or submissions.

### Supporting metrics

- Page-to-form-start rate.
- Form-start-to-valid-submission rate.
- Valid-submission-to-successful-record rate.
- Net-new lead conversion rate.
- Optional second-interest adoption.
- Validation-failure rate by field.
- Lead creation failure rate.
- New leads by primary interest and source.
- Downstream engagement by source and interest.
- Event, preview, tour, and eventual membership conversion by original source and interest.

Demographic profiling, session replay of sensitive form contents, and invasive fingerprinting are not required to evaluate this funnel.

## 18. Recommended Next Step for Visual Design

Create low-fidelity mobile-first wireframes based on this strategy before producing a polished visual system.

The wireframe phase should test:

1. Whether the value proposition is understood without relying on final copy or imagery.
2. Whether visitors can distinguish Strike from both an access-only gym and a fixed-format studio.
3. Whether pre-launch status is visible without overwhelming the main benefit.
4. Whether the CTA value exchange is clear before the visitor sees the form.
5. Whether the five interest choices are understandable and selectable in a few seconds.
6. Whether the optional second-interest interaction is obvious and enforces a two-interest maximum.
7. Whether trust, privacy, and unknown launch details are handled without creating a wall of caveats.
8. Whether one primary conversion remains visually dominant across desktop and mobile.

Recommended wireframe variants:

- **Variant A:** Compact hero with CTA that moves to a form after a short concept explanation.
- **Variant B:** Hero with the minimal form visible immediately, followed by explanation and repeated final CTA.

The variants should test information order and form timing, not different business promises. Usability review should include representatives of routine rebuilders, self-directed strength users, group-training prospects, and cautious beginners. Findings should determine the final page architecture before visual styling and final landing-page copy are developed.

## Final Recommendation

The first Strike landing page should ask for only what it can use immediately: first name, email, one required fitness interest, and an optional second interest. Last name and all additional qualification fields should be deferred.

This design creates a balanced first-touch exchange:

- Low enough friction to support conversion.
- Enough declared preference to make communication more relevant.
- Enough source and consent context to support responsible lifecycle marketing.
- No unnecessary personal or sensitive data.

The page should sell the value of staying informed, not pretend that Strike is already an operating club. Its strongest conversion advantage will be a clear problem-solution fit, a credible reason to engage before launch, and trust earned through accurate information rather than urgency or hype.
