# Strike Fitness audience strategy

**Status:** Proposed audience strategy for owner review. The broad local audience is established by Task 04; supporting segments and customer motivations remain hypotheses.

## 1. Purpose and evidence boundaries

Define prospective members and their information needs as reusable context for future landing page and email work. This document does not decide campaign execution, lead incentives, form fields, brand voice, or design.

Three evidence categories apply:

- **Established Strike decisions:** Task 04 specifies a broad local fitness audience. [Project decisions](../decisions.md) approve membership prices, benefit allocation, fees, terms, and Founders Rate. These approvals take precedence over older proposal labels in the [business document](strike-fitness.md).
- **Research-supported observations:** The [Crunch research](../research/crunch-lake-worth.md), particularly sections 4–7, documents advertised equipment, classes, coaching, amenities, and presale incentives. It supports the reference offering and promotional context, not customer demand or campaign effectiveness. Current membership evidence is separate from historical launch evidence.
- **Audience hypotheses:** All proposed needs, motivations, objections, and segments below are reasonable interpretations for review. Strike has no interviews, surveys, membership data, or campaign results. No segment size, demographic profile, or likely conversion rate is established.

Operating details still open in the business document remain conditional. No additional external research was conducted.

## 2. Primary target audience

Strike broadly seeks people for whom a local gym could become a practical part of regular life, across different experience levels and interests in strength, cardio, classes, coaching, and selected amenities. Prospective members could be starting, returning to, maintaining, or changing a fitness routine; none of these situations is verified as dominant.

Geographic relevance means a workable trip from home, work, or another regular destination. The business document proposes the broader Lake Worth area in suburban Palm Beach County, but this geography is unapproved. No address, travel radius, hours, or neighborhood priority should be inferred from Crunch's location.

The central audience hypothesis is that people will evaluate **convenience, useful access, and total membership value together**. Some may seek low-cost independent workouts; others may accept higher dues for activities they expect to use. Price sensitivity does not establish income, and fitness experience does not establish willingness to pay.

A new gym may attract people seeking another option, a fresh start, or presale savings. Others may want opening information before considering enrollment. Both are relevant to the lead-generation project: expressing interest is distinct from readiness to purchase. No age, gender, income, or single fitness niche defines the primary audience.

## 3. Customer needs and motivations

These motivations are hypotheses connected to the approved benefit allocation; operational delivery details remain open.

| Plausible need or desired outcome | Practical motivation and relevant benefit | Possible emotional motivation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build or maintain an exercise routine | Cardio, resistance machines, free weights, and independent functional/turf workouts across all tiers provide several ways to train. Suitability depends on hours and equipment availability. | Feel capable, regain momentum, or see personal progress. |
| Find value in activities actually used | Essential offers equipment access; Plus adds general classes; Complete adds specialty classes and selected amenities. Sauna and Wi-Fi are included across tiers. | Feel confident that the expense is worthwhile. |
| Exercise with instruction or structure | General classes are included in Plus/Complete; cycling, coached HIIT, and heated classes are included in Complete, subject to scheduled places. | Enjoy encouragement, variety, or a shared routine. |
| Get individualized help | Personal training is purchased separately on any tier. A free orientation has not been approved. | Reduce uncertainty or feel supported while learning. |
| Combine workouts with other conveniences | Complete includes water-massage beds, tanning, and one eligible accompanied guest per 24 hours; eligibility and session details remain open. | Enjoy the visit or companionship; no health outcome is guaranteed. |
| Reduce initial joining expense | Founders Rate reduces enrollment to $1, waives first-month dues/processing and the first scheduled annual fee, and includes a T-shirt and welcome bag. | Feel encouraged to begin or pleased to join early. |

Founders savings are one-time incentives, not a lifetime rate lock or proof that the membership will suit someone. Eligibility ends at the first 500 completed presale enrollments or opening, whichever comes first. An email lead does not qualify as an enrolled founder.

## 4. Barriers and objections

Different prospects may weigh these concerns differently. Their prevalence and importance are unknown.

| Potential objection | What the prospect may need to understand |
| --- | --- |
| “What will I actually pay?” | Dues are $9.99/$24.99/$29.99; with processing, monthly charges are $12.24/$27.24/$32.24 before tax, plus the separate $59.99 annual fee. Standard enrollment is $75. Founders waivers do not remove later charges; its next annual fee starts in month 15. |
| “What if I stop using it?” | Essential has a 12-period minimum; Plus/Complete have three-period minimums, then monthly renewal. Ordinary cancellation requires 30 days' notice and cannot take effect before the minimum ends. The free founder month counts toward that minimum. |
| “Will it open when expected?” | No Strike opening date is approved. Approved terms move activation and billing with the actual opening; pre-activation cancellation refunds all collected amounts. A forecast must not be presented as a completed milestone. |
| “Can I use the activities I want?” | Facility presence and membership inclusion differ. Classes require a scheduled place; no reservation is guaranteed. Class timetables, equipment quantities, and amenity limits remain unresolved. |
| “Will it fit my day?” | Confirmed location, operating hours, and class times would help assess convenience. Childcare, multi-club access, and 24-hour operation cannot be promised. |
| “Will I feel comfortable or trust the business?” | Explain available activities, support, charges, and launch status concretely. Learning concerns, perceived intimidation, or reluctance to buy before seeing the gym are plausible, not observed. Do not invent testimonials or imply Crunch affiliation. |

These four proposed segments overlap. They describe needs, not exclusive identities, ranked priorities, or automatic tier preferences. Budget, convenience, experience, and launch confidence cut across all four.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 6. Cross-channel messaging implications

Future landing page and email communications should use this shared audience understanding without turning it into a campaign plan:

- **Make relevance concrete:** Explain the workout options and practical information people need to judge fit. Variety should remain understandable without requiring identification with one niche.
- **Explain value through access and cost:** Pair benefits with tier eligibility and distinguish dues from mandatory fees, minimum terms, and optional purchases.
- **Build confidence with accurate status:** Separate approved inclusions from unresolved operational details and planned facilities from delivered facilities. Do not imply guaranteed class access or results.
- **Keep savings precise:** Describe Founders Rate as temporary joining savings with continuing charges and eligibility conditions. The 500-enrollment limit does not establish demand, remaining availability, or facility capacity.
- **Respect different readiness levels:** Clarify whether an action expresses interest or completes enrollment. Do not imply that email signup reserves a rate or qualifies for merchandise.
- **Allow overlapping interests:** Relevance can come from several activities. Avoid presenting a single interest as a fixed identity, membership recommendation, or purchase intention.

## 7. Considerations for future CRM segmentation

Strategic segments organize proposed needs for communication planning. CRM segments would group actual records using available, appropriately collected data. A lead's stated fitness interest can provide a relevance signal without validating the entire strategic profile.

The project already anticipates primary and secondary fitness interests. Voluntary information could eventually reveal stated activity preferences and, if later collection is approved, goals, availability preferences, or readiness to join. These are possible learning areas, not proposed form fields or a data model.

Price sensitivity, intimidation, motivation, travel convenience, and intended usage remain hypotheses unless directly explored. Stated interest still does not establish behavior, purchasing power, tier preference, or enrollment. Preserve room for multiple and changing interests; this document defines no CRM rules, ESP, or workflows.

## 8. Assumptions and questions for owner review

1. **Local relevance:** Is the proposed Lake Worth-area geography the intended setting? Exact location and access hours remain necessary to make convenience claims concrete.
2. **Segment usefulness:** Do these four overlapping needs adequately represent the desired breadth? No segment dominance or demographic targeting is recommended without further direction or evidence.
3. **Value hypothesis:** Do total cost, useful activities, and routine fit provide the right starting emphasis? Their relative influence, and the appeal of Founders savings, are unvalidated.
4. **Support and availability:** What instruction, class scheduling, equipment access, and amenity restrictions will eventually be supportable? These open operational decisions affect confidence without changing approved benefits.
5. **Readiness to act:** How much reassurance might prospects need before expressing interest versus enrolling? Treat pre-opening uncertainty as a hypothesis to explore, not proof of reluctance.

Owner review is the next checkpoint. Lead incentives, capture fields, campaign strategy, and execution remain for later tasks.
